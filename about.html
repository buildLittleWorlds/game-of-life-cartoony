First things first

A game that plays itself

The Game of Life is a cellular automaton: a grid of cells that all follow the same local rules. You choose the opening pattern, start the clock, and become a spectator. Every later generation is determined completely by the one before it.

That simplicity is the magic. A few cells can vanish, settle into a tiny sculpture, pulse forever, travel across the board, or build structures complicated enough to perform computation.

Every cell watches its 8 neighbors.

A playful experiment

How Conway found the balance

In the late 1960s, British mathematician John Horton Conway became interested in whether very simple rules could create open-ended, unpredictable behavior. He and colleagues tried candidate rules by hand with counters on grid-like boards. Conway later remembered the search as about 18 months of “coffee times”—informal sessions of testing, watching, and adjusting.

He was not trying to imitate biology precisely. He wanted a miniature mathematical universe poised between two dull extremes: everything dying immediately and everything exploding uncontrollably.

  1. 1No obviously inevitable, unlimited explosion.
  2. 2Some patterns that seemed capable of growing without limit.
  3. 3Small patterns that could evolve for a long time before dying, stabilizing, or repeating.

Conway shared the result with mathematical-games writer Martin Gardner. Gardner introduced Life to a wide audience in the October 1970 issue of Scientific American, where he summed up the goal neatly: the population’s behavior should be difficult to predict.

B3 / S23

The rules of Life

Count the living cells in the eight spaces around each cell. Then update every cell at the same time.

2–3

Survival

A living cell with two or three living neighbors stays alive.

3

Birth

A dead cell with exactly three living neighbors becomes alive.

0–1

Isolation

A living cell with fewer than two living neighbors dies.

4–8

Crowding

A living cell with more than three living neighbors dies.

Why “B3/S23”? It is compact notation for birth with 3 neighbors and survival with 2 or 3. Any other cell is dead in the next generation.
